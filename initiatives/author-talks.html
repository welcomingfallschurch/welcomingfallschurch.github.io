Author Talks

Building a Community of Neighbors through the power of story.

Welcoming Falls Church—in partnership with the City of Falls Church, Mary Riley Styles Public Library, Falls Church City Public Schools (FCCPS), the Falls Church Education Foundation, Happy Endings Hospitality, and Viet Place Collective—is thrilled to announce that acclaimed author Viet Thanh Nguyen will visit Falls Church on March 5–6, 2027 for a dynamic, multi-generational cultural program.

Professor Nguyen is the author of the Pulitzer Prize–winning novel The Sympathizer (adapted into an HBO miniseries) along with numerous other widely praised literary and non-fiction works. He was honored as Harvard University’s Charles Eliot Norton Lecturer in Poetry for 2023–2024, joining a century-long tradition of world-renowned figures in the arts. Hosting a literary figure of his stature marks a major cultural event for Falls Church, offering the community a unique window on storytelling, identity, and the refugee experience.

Professor Nguyen will engage our community across a two-day residency:

  • Student Engagement Program (Friday, March 5): A dedicated session and literary dialogue for students and faculty at Meridian High School, co-hosted alongside FCCPS and the Falls Church Education Foundation.
  • Public Keynote Address & Community Reception (Saturday, March 6): We invite you to join us to welcome Professor Nguyen for a public keynote address exploring the refugee experience.

We warmly invite book clubs and community members to read his work ahead of time. Mary Riley Styles Public Library will co-sponsor public book club discussions leading up to the visit, making this a true community-wide read.

Join Us: Please register to receive additional information about his upcoming visit including details about Professor Nguyen's public talk as well as how to get involved in public book club discussions. If you register now, we will send you an invitation to join the public talk taking place on Saturday, March 6 (time TBC). You can also follow our Activity Calendar for forthcoming details.

Past Visits: A Legacy of Connection

Welcoming Week 2024: Sindya Bhanoo (Seeking Fortune Elsewhere)

In September 2024, Welcoming Falls Church hosted award-winning author and professor Sindya Bhanoo. Her visit coincided with the seventh annual proclamation of Welcoming Week, highlighting our ongoing commitment to being a welcoming community.

Ms. Bhanoo discussed her collection of short stories, Seeking Fortune Elsewhere, which explores the South Asian immigrant experience. Her visit was a community-wide event, featuring:

  • Public Engagement: A public talk at Mary Riley Styles Public Library focused on essential themes of hospitality, immigration, and our shared goal of building a "community of neighbors."
  • Meridian High School: A specialized visit and workshop for students encouraged the next generation of writers to find their voice. Ms. Bhanoo, who writes for the Washington Post and the New York Times, was engaging and dynamic with the student audience.

Fall 2020: Alan Gratz (Refugee)

During the pandemic, we used a virtual format to host Alan Gratz, the best-selling author of Refugee. Over 250 households tied into this landmark event on Zoom for a dialogue and stories about global refugee crises, with Mr. Gratz sharing interlocking stories of displacement from 1930s Germany, 1990s Cuba, and 2010s Syria.

Welcoming Week 2019: Imbolo Mbue (Behold the Dreamers)

Our inaugural Welcoming Week featured a community read and talk by Imbolo Mbue, author of the Oprah’s Book Club selection Behold the Dreamers. This milestone event featured multiple community engagements:

  • George Mason High School (since renamed Meridian High School): Ms. Mbue presented a talk on "Immigration & the American Dream" for the student body and the broader public, hosted by the Falls Church City Public Schools. Then-FCCPS Superintendent Peter Noonan had recommended the book as a community read in Summer 2019, and the event was well-attended by members of the community, parents, teachers, and students alike.
  • Tysons-Pimmitt Regional Library: A second public talk and book signing cosponsored with the Friends of Tysons-Pimmitt Regional Library, welcoming neighbors from across the regional area.

Ms. Mbue’s visit was particularly resonant as she has long-standing ties to Falls Church, where she lived and worked before her literary career took her to New York. Sharing her story at George Mason High School and at Tyson-Pimmitt Regional Library allowed our community to connect with an author who understands the unique local landscape of the Mid-Atlantic immigrant experience.
